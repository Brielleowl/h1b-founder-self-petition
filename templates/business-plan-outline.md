# Business plan outline (Exhibit E)

A USCIS reader is checking three things: the company is real and will operate; the position's work
is needed to build the product; the company can pay the wage for the whole period. Write to those,
in future tense for anything not yet shipped, 15–25 pages, with a correct table of contents and page
numbers (generate the PDF from source — Word's exported TOC drifts).

Numbers must match the support letter, the board consent and `case.json`: contribution amount,
bank balance and date, revenue start, raise amount and month, period cost of the position, fee total.

## Cover
Company name, "Business Plan", date (on or before the filing date), "Prepared for U.S. Citizenship and
Immigration Services in support of Form I-129". Confidential marking optional.

## 1. Executive summary (one page)
Who, what, for whom, why now; the position being petitioned and why the company needs it; funding
in one sentence; the 5-year revenue line in one sentence.

## 2. Company overview
Legal form, incorporation date and state, foreign qualification, EIN, worksite (home office is fine;
quote the lease clause that permits business use), officers and directors, ownership, bank account.
Mission. Founder background in two paragraphs — only the employers the founder wants named, no
third-party confidential detail.

## 3. Market
Problem; market size table with citations (analyst forecasts, named reports); growth drivers; recent
acquisitions or funding rounds in the segment as evidence of demand (table with source for each);
target customers and buyer.

## 4. Competition
Table of 4–6 competitors / substitutes: what they do, price point, gap the company fills.

## 5. Product and roadmap
What the product will do, in future tense; architecture at one-diagram depth; roadmap by quarter for
the petition period; how the petitioned position's duties map to the roadmap (cite the JD duty
numbers).

## 6. Pricing and go-to-market
Pricing tiers with the unit of charge; comparison table with market prices; first customers and the
sales motion the founder will run in the owner-duty hours.

## 7. Operations
Worksite, tools, contractors, compliance (data handling), hiring plan by month with roles and when
each starts.

## 8. Management and governance
Director(s), officers, ownership %; that the founder is the petitioned employee under the
beneficiary-owner rule; time split (specialty vs owner duties) — same numbers as the JD.

## 9. Financial plan
- **Funding of the first 18 months (sources and uses table):** sources = founder contribution +
  planned revenue + planned raise; uses = USCIS fees, wages + employer taxes for the position,
  other overhead, contractors/marketing, delivery costs; ending cash.
- **Five-year projection table:** revenue, COGS, headcount, payroll, other opex, net, ending cash.
- **Assumptions** listed explicitly (price, customers per quarter, hire months).
- **Sensitivity / contingency:** at least "raise delayed" and "revenue delayed" cases with the
  shortfall and the response (defer hires, founder contribution). The wage is paid in every case.
- **Return profile** (optional, one table) if the plan leans on investability.

## 10. The position
Title, SOC, LCA number, hours, wage, period — identical to the JD. Why the product cannot be built
without this work.

## 11. Conclusion
Three sentences.

## Sources
Numbered list; every market figure in the body carries a [n].

## Appendix (optional)
Website screenshots, product mockups, letters of intent, lease excerpt.
